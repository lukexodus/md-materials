## Code of Conduct


A Code of Conduct (CoC) is a governance document that establishes the social contract of the community. It protects the project's health by defining acceptable behavior and the consequences of harassment or exclusion. It is a requirement for many open-source foundations (e.g., Apache, Linux Foundation) and corporate open-source policies.

Scope and Applicability

The CoC must clearly state where it applies.

- **Spaces:** Issue trackers, pull requests, mailing lists, project chat channels (Slack/Discord), and public events where the individual represents the project.
    
- **Duration:** It applies to all interactions, regardless of time or private/public status, if the interaction impacts the project community.
    

Standards of Behavior

Explicitly list examples of positive and negative behavior to remove ambiguity.

- **Positive:** Using welcoming language, being respectful of differing viewpoints, accepting constructive criticism, and showing empathy.
    
- **Negative:** Sexualized language or imagery, personal attacks (trolling/insulting), public or private harassment, publishing others' private information (doxing), and other conduct reasonably considered inappropriate in a professional setting.
    

Enforcement Responsibilities

Define who is responsible for enforcing the rules (typically the Project Maintainers or a dedicated Conduct Committee).

- **Bias and Recusal:** If a maintainer is involved in a report, they must recuse themselves from the decision-making process regarding that specific incident.
    
- **Confidentiality:** Reports must be kept confidential to protect the victim.
    

Enforcement Guidelines (The Correction Matrix)

A CoC without enforcement mechanisms is ineffective. Define the escalation ladder:

1. **Correction:** A private, written warning for minor, unintentional infractions. (e.g., unintentional use of non-inclusive language).
    
2. **Warning:** A warning with consequences for continued behavior. (e.g., deleting comments, temporary mute).
    
3. **Temporary Ban:** A suspension from interaction for a defined period (e.g., 1 week to 1 month).
    
4. **Permanent Ban:** Permanent removal from the community for serious or repeated violations.
    

Attribution

Most projects do not write a CoC from scratch. They adopt widely accepted standards such as the Contributor Covenant (used by Linux, Google, Microsoft, Apple, etc.). This ensures the wording is legally and socially vetted.

**Key Points**

- **Safety:** The primary goal is psychological safety, which fosters better collaboration and higher code quality.
    
- **Visibility:** Link the CoC in the `README.md` and `CONTRIBUTING.md`.
    
- **Actionability:** Provide a clear email address or reporting form for incidents.
    

**Example**

> "We as members, contributors, and leaders pledge to make participation in our community a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression..." (_Excerpt from Contributor Covenant v2.1_)

Next Steps
